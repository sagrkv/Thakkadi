## How do you calculate court fees in Karnataka?

First identify the proceeding and its valuation rule. When **Schedule I, Article 1** applies, select the row containing the subject-matter value and calculate **base fee + rate × (value − threshold)**. For example, a money claim of ₹1,00,000 gives ₹4,875 + 7% × ₹25,000 = **₹6,625**.

[Open the Karnataka Court Fee Calculator](/court-fee-calculator) to choose a supported suit type and see its calculation breakdown. Property value, the amount claimed, and the value of a plaintiff's share can lead to different valuation bases; they are not interchangeable.

Use the base fee stated in the matching row. It already accounts for the lower ranges: do not add all the rows again or apply the selected rate to the entire claim.

## Worked examples for money claims

These examples assume the amount claimed is the subject-matter value and Article 1 applies.

- **₹10,000 claim:** 2.5% × ₹10,000 = **₹250**.
- **₹50,000 claim:** ₹375 + 7.5% × (₹50,000 − ₹15,000) = **₹3,000**.
- **₹1,00,000 claim:** ₹4,875 + 7% × (₹1,00,000 − ₹75,000) = **₹6,625**.
- **₹45,00,000 claim:** ₹1,77,125 + 2.5% × ₹5,00,000 = **₹1,89,625**.
- **₹65,00,000 claim:** ₹2,22,125 + 1.5% × ₹5,00,000 = **₹2,29,625**.
- **₹90,00,000 claim:** ₹2,47,125 + 0.5% × ₹10,00,000 = **₹2,52,125**.

The calculator rounds displayed fees up to whole rupees. Check the applicable valuation, exemptions, and rounding requirements before filing.

## Property suits, fixed fees, and appeals

The Article 1 table does not by itself decide the value to enter. Declaratory suits, injunctions, partition suits, and other proceedings have their own valuation provisions. Some proceedings instead attract fixed fees or a separate schedule.

An appeal can involve a different amount in dispute from the original claim. Choose the supported proceeding in the calculator and check the relevant provision, rather than copying the original fee automatically.
